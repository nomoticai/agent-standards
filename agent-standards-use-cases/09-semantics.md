# 9. Semantics (SEM)

## Scope, status, and terminology

This document is a problem statement, not a solution proposal. Its subject is **shared meaning for coordination acts**: how two agents with no prior relationship can express, interpret, and later prove what was requested, offered, accepted, refused, and committed. It argues that this layer today exists only as per-stack conventions, bilateral contracts, or per-message language interpretation — and that each fails in a distinct, observable way. It stops short of prescribing a vocabulary, an encoding, or an ontology.

One distinction, borrowed from speech-act theory because the prior art below is built on it, carries this document's structure: the **coordination act** (that this message is a query, a proposal, an acceptance, a refusal, a commitment) versus the **propositional content** (what is being queried, proposed, or committed to — the items, prices, terms, and conditions). The claims this document can honestly make differ sharply between the two layers, and conflating them is precisely the mistake its most important predecessor made.

**Boundary declarations.** *Communication (07)* owns the carriage of intent: the envelope, the intent *slot*, its structural separation from content, and the cryptographic binding of both to sender and lineage. This document is what 07 declared it would hand forward: what legitimately fills the slot. *Tools (10)* owns capability and function description — what an agent or tool can do; this document owns what agents *say to each other* about what they want done. *Session (08)* hands this document its declared receivable: session states (suspension, resumption, compensation, succession) need expressible acts. *Discovery (06)* hands forward entity-level capability negotiation, which is conducted *in* the acts described here. And a reaffirmation owed to *delegation (04)*: an act that references a mandate does not make the mandate's purpose decidable; that boundary survives this document untouched.

## The skeptical case

Structure description is a mature field. OpenAPI and JSON Schema specify REST contracts to the field level; Protobuf and gRPC give typed, versioned message shapes; GraphQL types entire query surfaces; MCP tool descriptions and A2A agent cards give machine-readable accounts of what agents can do. The Semantic Web went further and built general shared-meaning infrastructure — RDF, OWL, SPARQL — for anyone who wanted it. And beneath everything sits the modern trump card: agents *are* language models. Two agents can exchange plain prose and interpret it on either side, with no schema at all. Between mature contract tooling on one side and universal language interpretation on the other, the skeptic asks what could possibly be missing in the middle.

## Where the mismatch actually lies

Both of the skeptic's pillars are real, and each fails on its own terms at the layer between them.

**Schemas describe structure, not commitment.** An OpenAPI spec says a POST to /orders takes a customer_id and an items array. It does not say what processing the request *commits the seller to*, when money moves, what is cancellable and until when, or how this operation relates to a differently shaped POST to /purchases elsewhere that performs essentially the same act. The vocabulary differences across platforms — one calls it "buy," another "purchase," another "reserve then commit" — are not naming trivia: **the words encode different commitments**, and translating between them is translating obligations, which is exactly where translation is lossy and disputes are born. Under the schema path, every pair of organizations negotiates or reverse-engineers this commitment layer bilaterally, and integration cost grows with the product of participants.

**Language interpretation carries meaning and destroys its properties.** The prose path avoids bilateral contracts and pays for it four ways: inference cost on every message; non-deterministic interpretation that occasionally executes the wrong act; the injection exposure of document 07 — natural-language intent *is* the in-band channel where content can become instruction; and, most consequentially, evidentiary collapse. When a dispute arises, the record of what was requested and agreed consists of two independently generated prose messages and whatever each side's parser made of them — document 04's mandate-as-prompt-text failure, recurring at the transaction level. And none of this makes language interpretation dispensable: it is why agents exist, and it remains the only mechanism for content that cannot be pre-structured. The realistic question is not whether meaning travels as structure or prose, but which layer of meaning must be structural for coordination to be dispatchable, bindable, and adjudicable — and that layer is the act layer.

**The act-layer claim now has controlled empirical support.** A 7,200-trial benchmark across four model families (Hood, 2026) measured endpoint selection when intent-aligned act verbs (BOOK, FIND, QUERY) compete with conventional REST/CRUD interfaces in a single catalog: frontier models selected correctly 10–29 percentage points more often on intent-named endpoints (aggregate p < 0.001), and three independent ablations triangulate the mechanism as the *name itself*, not documentation quality — crossing descriptions collapsed CRUD selection by 39–43 points to near-chance, while intent-named endpoints largely survived the same swap, and one frontier model's selection accuracy *improved* 13 points when verbose documentation was removed. The intent density of the act encoding is therefore a measured engineering variable, not a stylistic preference: a name that encodes the act survives documentation noise; a generic verb delegates all meaning to its description and fails with it. Two boundary results are imported with the finding and discipline it. First, parametric accuracy collapsed without documentation (as low as 38% from 80%) even as selection held — which is this document's act/content split, *measured*: names suffice for recognizing the act and are entirely insufficient for constructing the content, so the FIPA relocation problem now has data on both of its halves. Second, the entire effect vanished at small model scale (~3B parameters, 0-point gap): the act signal exists only where the consumer can decode it — document 05's EV-consumability lesson in its most literal form.

The Semantic Web's fate, honestly told in earlier drafts and retained here, marks the far boundary: general-purpose shared meaning through community ontologies required coordination that never materialized, spawned ontology-matching as a research field, and lost to bespoke schemas that individual parties controlled. Twenty-five years on, the web runs on structure without shared meaning. The lesson is scope: whatever is claimable here is far smaller than "shared meaning" — and the prior art shows exactly how much smaller.

## What observably breaks today

**Every stack's act layer is private.** MCP expresses acts through its method surface, A2A through task semantics, function-calling frameworks through per-deployment tool names. The same coordination act — request this, propose that, decline — has a different vocabulary in every stack, so document 07's bridges translate not just formats but *acts*, lossily, and cross-stack negotiation inherits every mismatch.

**First contact costs an integration or a gamble.** Two agents that have never interacted either require a pre-negotiated contract (returning to the pairwise world, cost scaling with the product of participants) or open with prose and accept the interpretation risk on a counterparty they have no history with — the worst possible place for non-determinism.

**Disputes are adjudicated over prompts.** When the wrong item ships, the wrong quantity is billed, or a commitment is denied, there is no common, verifiable expression of what was requested and agreed — only natural-language messages and framework-specific payloads. The audit layer this suite keeps requiring has nothing semantic to audit.

**Refusal is unstructured.** Declining, partially accepting, and conditionally accepting are rendered as prose. Receivers retry refused requests, misread conditions as acceptances, and cannot produce an auditable record that a request was declined — an act as consequential as acceptance, with none of its machinery.

**Confidence is least informative exactly where it is most needed.** When act encoding and description conflict — the condition every lossy bridge, stale integration, and poisoned description produces — measured model confidence decouples from accuracy catastrophically: in the benchmark above, models reported roughly 88% confidence while achieving roughly 30% accuracy under description mismatch (+60–67 points of overconfidence). Escalation and retry logic keyed on reported confidence therefore under-escalates precisely in the failure case — the agent is most certain when most wrong. Intent-dense act names partially re-anchor calibration by supplying a second, corroborating signal the description cannot override. This gives the tools document's description-integrity requirement (10) measured safety stakes: a drifted or poisoned description does not merely mislead — it produces maximally confident wrong dispatch.

**Multi-step negotiation is a per-platform state machine.** Query, propose, counter, accept, commit flows exist in every marketplace and none share the states or the transitions; an agent that negotiates on two platforms implements two negotiation protocols.

**Session acts are inexpressible.** Suspension, resumption, compensation, and succession — the states document 08 defined by need — have no common expression, so the workflows that need them most (long-running, multi-party, partially failed) coordinate their most delicate transitions in the least shared vocabulary.

## Properties without a common definition

Framed as what previously unacquainted parties must be able to establish, stated at the act layer where they are claimable and honestly bounded at the content layer where they are not:

- **Act recognition without negotiation:** the receiver can determine *which coordination act* a message performs — query, propose, accept, refuse, commit, cancel, and the session acts of document 08 — without a bilateral contract and without inference. (This is the dispatch half of determinism, and the only half on offer.)
- **Commitment clarity:** what an act obligates — what accepting a proposal binds, when a commitment attaches, what refusal forecloses — defined at the act layer rather than divergently implied per platform.
- **Content discipline, not content determinism:** the domain content an act carries (items, prices, terms) can be typed and governed by domain vocabularies, with the governing vocabulary *identified in the message* — but its meaning is compressed and administered, never made deterministic; residual ambiguity is adjudicated, not eliminated.
- **Bindable expression:** the act and its content are expressible in a form document 07's envelope can bind to sender and lineage, so the transaction record is evidence rather than reconstruction. *(Binding mechanism owned by 07; what gets bound is owned here.)*
- **Auditable negative acts:** refusal and conditional acceptance carry the same structure, binding, and auditability as affirmative acts.
- **Extension without base fragmentation:** domains add vocabulary under a discipline that preserves base-act interoperability — a requirement stated with its known failure mode attached (below), not as a solved property.
- **Version coexistence:** parties on different vocabulary versions can still recognize each other's base acts.

## Prior art, and what it constrains

**FIPA-ACL and KQML** are the obligatory confrontation, because this document's earlier drafts reinvented them without citation. The FIPA performative set — query-if, propose, accept-proposal, reject-proposal, request, agree, refuse, confirm, inform — *is* the closed coordination-verb vocabulary, grounded in speech-act theory, typed, wire-embedded, extensible, standardized in the late 1990s, and never deployed beyond academia. Its failure teaches two lessons that structure this entire document. First, the act layer was the buildable part; FIPA's formal semantics (modal BDI logic defining what each performative "meant") were never implemented or verified by anything, so even inside the standard, meaning was convention. Second and deeper: **the performative wraps a content language, and the content still required shared ontology** — FIPA-SL, ontology services, the whole apparatus — so the hard problem was not solved by the verb layer; it was relocated inside it. "PROPOSE with typed parameters" conceals the same move: *price* (which currency? tax treatment? delivery terms?) is where the meaning lives. Any proposal here inherits FIPA's structure and must state what it does about FIPA's relocation problem.

**HTTP methods** remain the strongest success analogy, with its mechanism named: the methods work because they are *content-agnostic* — GET retrieves the representation whatever the resource is; the semantics are deliberately thin. The analogy therefore supports exactly the act layer and warns against exactly the domain layer: the further a vocabulary reaches past thin coordination acts into business meaning, the further it leaves HTTP's success conditions and enters EDI's.

**EDI (X12, EDIFACT)** is the giant deployed precedent for shared *business* semantics: the 850/ORDERS purchase order and its siblings have moved commerce for fifty years. EDI proves shared transaction vocabulary is achievable at civilization scale — and proves it does not eliminate bilateral interpretation: every trading-partner relationship still runs on implementation guides that pin down what the standardized segments mean *for this pair*. The negotiation was not removed; it was compressed and relocated. That is the honest ceiling for domain content, and **ebXML** — the XML-era attempt to rebuild EDI's layer alongside document 07's WS-\* stack — marks where more ambition went to die.

**schema.org** is the modern adoption lesson: a shared vocabulary that actually won, because a dominant consumer (search engines) rewarded every increment of adoption. Shared meaning spreads where incentives pay per participant; FIPA had no such consumer. **ISO 20022** shows shared financial-message semantics achieving adoption at treaty-and-decades pace. **FHIR's profile system** — inherited from document 08 — is the extension-model warning: profiles built to specialize a shared base fragmented interoperability across them, rhyming with document 01's XMPP/XEP lesson; extension discipline is a requirement precisely because extension mechanisms have repeatedly become the fragmentation they were meant to prevent.

**Aviation English** is retained from earlier drafts and promoted from analogy to prior art: ICAO standard phraseology is a real closed coordination vocabulary for safety-critical use among strangers — achieved through treaty-level governance, enforced training, and continuous tightening. Its honest edge belongs in the record: Tenerife, 1977 — the deadliest accident in aviation history — turned in part on the non-standard "we are now at takeoff," and the response was harder governance of the vocabulary, not its abandonment. Closed vocabularies fail at their edges; the question a solution must answer is who governs the edge. **Traffic signs and musical notation** stay as the accessible illustrations they are, with the same governance footnote: both took international convention regimes (Vienna Convention; centuries of practice) to converge.

The constraint the history imposes: the act layer has been standardized before and died of no adoption incentive (FIPA); the domain layer has been standardized before and lives with permanent bilateral residue (EDI); general shared meaning has been attempted and lost to bespoke control (Semantic Web); extension mechanisms have repeatedly refragmented what they extended (XEP, FHIR profiles). A solution must place itself against all four, and its adoption story must name its schema.org — the consumer that pays for compliance.

## The core use case: a purchase between strangers

An assistant agent at Company A must buy office supplies for its principal, within a budget. Discovery (06) surfaces a supplier agent at Company B. The two agents have never interacted; the companies have never integrated. The coordination that must occur is a short negotiation: the assistant asks about availability and price, the supplier proposes, the assistant accepts within budget, the supplier commits, and both sides need a record of exactly that.

**What breaks today — path one, the contract.** Before coordinating, the assistant's operators read Company B's API specification, learn its operation names, field vocabulary, and response shapes, and adapt — work repeated for every supplier, mirrored by Company B for every buyer, scaling as the product. And the contract specifies structure, not commitment: whether Company B's "reserve" charges the card, and when its "commit" becomes cancellable, is learned from documentation prose or from incidents.

**What breaks today — path two, the conversation.** The assistant sends "I'd like to purchase 10 units of ABC-123 at your listed price"; the supplier's model interprets, proposes in prose; the assistant's model interprets back. Each hop costs inference and admits misreading; the supplier's prose is an injectable band (07); and when the wrong item arrives, the evidentiary record of the transaction is two generated messages that neither side's substrate ever bound to anything. The dispute is adjudicated by archaeology over prompts.

**What any adequate answer must make possible.** The assistant's opening act is recognizable *as a query* and the supplier's response *as a proposal* by any compliant party, with no prior contract — and acceptance, refusal, and commitment are equally recognizable, equally structured, equally bindable to sender and lineage through document 07's envelope, so the transaction record shows what was requested and agreed as verifiable acts rather than parsed prose. The domain content — items, prices, terms — is carried under an identified governing vocabulary, so both sides know *which* compressed meaning applies and what remains for adjudication.

**What no answer can promise, and this document does not claim.** Deterministic domain meaning: FIPA's relocation problem and EDI's implementation-guide residue stand; content semantics are governed and compressed, never eliminated as an interpretation problem. Dispute-free commerce: shared acts do not remove disputes — they make disputes *adjudicable*, by giving both parties one referenceable record of the acts performed; this is document 04's conclusion (recorded, referenceable, disputable — not decidable) arriving at the transaction layer. Frictionless extension: domain vocabularies will specialize, and the fragmentation history (XEP, FHIR profiles) applies with full force. The displacement of language: prose remains the payload for everything that cannot be pre-structured, per document 07; the claim covers the act layer and the discipline of the content layer, nothing more. Uniform benefit across consumers: the measured gains are capability-conditional — absent below a model-scale threshold — so the act layer's value, like every signal in this suite, depends on the consumer's capacity to decode it, and deployments below the threshold need scaffolding (pre-filtered catalogs, constrained selection) more than they need vocabulary.

**Open questions this use case exposes.** The size and membership of the base act set (FIPA's ~20 versus HTTP's ~9 is a real design tension between coverage and universality); the boundary between base coordination acts and domain acts — the measured benchmark's verb list mixes the two (QUERY and CHECK beside BOOK and REFUND), and a tiered vocabulary (small mandatory core, governed extension registry) is the natural resolution the act/content split implies; the governance of domain vocabularies (who plays ICAO); the adoption engine (who plays the search engines of schema.org); and a measured bootstrap cost at the discovery seam — current embedding models, trained on REST-vocabulary corpora, surface intent-named endpoints *less* reliably than CRUD-named ones, so the act layer's discoverability lags its selectability until retrieval corpora catch up — all solution-space; the failures stand regardless.

## Additional semantics needs

Stated as observable needs or failures; items owned elsewhere are tagged.

**First contact**
- Complete a multi-step negotiation (query → propose → accept/refuse → commit) with a party never previously encountered, with each step recognized as its act without a prior contract and without inference.
- Conduct entity-level capability negotiation — the fine-grained phase received from discovery (06) — in acts both parties recognize.
- Dispatch on the act at measured, benchmarkable accuracy: act selection is now an evaluable property with controlled methodology, and a shared act layer makes the evaluation portable across stacks rather than re-run per private vocabulary.
- Keep reported confidence informative under encoding/description mismatch, so escalation and retry logic keyed on confidence does not under-escalate in exactly the failure case. *(Measured stakes for 10's description integrity.)*

**Commitment and record**
- Establish, from the record alone, what was requested, what was proposed, and what was agreed — as bound acts, not reconstructed prose. *(Binding via 07; consumed by audit.)*
- Determine what an acceptance obligated and when a commitment attached, at the act layer, uniformly across platforms.

**Negative and conditional acts**
- Refuse a request such that the refusal is structured, bound, auditable, and terminal — not a prose reply to be retried.
- Accept conditionally such that the condition is part of the recorded act, not an interpretive gloss on it.

**Session and lifecycle acts** *(receiving document 08's handoff)*
- Express suspension, resumption, compensation, and participant succession as recognized acts, so the most delicate transitions of long-running work stop being the least shared.
- Express cancellation as an act with defined scope — which composes with transport's propagation (01) and authorization's effect-time (03) rather than replacing them.

**Coexistence and evolution**
- Recognize a counterparty's base acts across vocabulary versions.
- Surface intent-named acts through embedding-based discovery as reliably as REST-named ones — a measured gap today, since retrieval corpora skew toward REST vocabulary. *(Seam with 06.)*
- Carry domain content under an identified governing vocabulary, so "which meaning regime applies" is part of the message rather than part of the archaeology.
- Bridge between the shared act layer and a legacy vocabulary (an EDI trading relationship, a platform API) with the loss explicit rather than silent.

## What this document does not claim

- **Not** a verb set — though the bar for alternatives has risen. The closed-vocabulary shape is a strong candidate with the best success analogy (HTTP methods), the most instructive corpse (FIPA), and now controlled empirical support for its mechanism: intent-dense names measurably outperform generic verbs at frontier scale and resist documentation noise that collapses generic ones. The problem statement still does not mandate the shape; it requires that any alternative — typed interaction patterns, a shared negotiation core — demonstrate equivalent intent-signal density under the same conditions. The evidence is imported with its own stated limits: synthetic catalog, single-turn selection, English-only tasks, single-rater rubric, and a capability threshold estimated rather than measured.
- **Not** deterministic meaning for domain content. The act layer is claimable; the content layer is governable and compressible, with permanent bilateral and adjudicative residue — EDI's fifty-year testimony is accepted as the ceiling.
- **Not** immunity from extension fragmentation. Discipline is required precisely because extension mechanisms have historically become the fragmentation; a solution owes a governance answer, not a mechanism alone.
- **Not** the displacement of natural language, which remains the content modality of record for the unstructurable, per document 07 — and remains an interpretation cost wherever it carries what could have been an act.
- **Not** that shared acts prevent disputes or make purposes decidable. They make transactions adjudicable and leave document 04's purpose boundary exactly where that document drew it.

The claim is: the coordination-act layer — what a message *does* between strangers: query, propose, accept, refuse, commit, cancel, suspend, resume, compensate — has no common definition across agent stacks; the two available substitutes fail measurably in opposite directions (bilateral contracts scale as the product of participants; per-message interpretation costs inference, admits misexecution and injection, and collapses the evidentiary record to prose); disputes over agent transactions are consequently adjudicated over prompts; and every one of these failures compounds with exactly the stranger-to-stranger, cross-organization coordination the rest of this suite describes.

## Forward and backward dependencies

- **Communication (07), backward:** fills the intent slot declared there, under the FIPA warning attached at the handoff; binding of acts to sender and lineage remains 07's mechanism; the act/content split here parallels and composes with 07's envelope/payload split.
- **Session (08), backward:** receives the session-state vocabulary requirement; suspension, resumption, compensation, and succession appear above as acts to be expressible.
- **Discovery (06), backward:** receives entity-level capability negotiation into the act layer — and hands back a measured bootstrap cost: embedding-based discovery currently surfaces intent-named endpoints less reliably than REST-named ones, so act-layer adoption carries a discoverability lag at that seam.
- **Authorization (03) and Delegation (04), backward:** acts reference chains and mandates without altering their semantics; refusal and conditional acceptance interact with grant checking; the purpose-decidability boundary of 04 is reaffirmed untouched; cancellation acts compose with 03's effect-time constraints.
- **Tools (10), forward:** receives capability and function description — what a tool or agent can do — as distinct from the acts agents perform toward each other; the boundary is declared here for that document to honor or renegotiate. The description-integrity requirement declared there acquires measured stakes here: encoding/description mismatch produces not just wrong dispatch but maximally *confident* wrong dispatch (+60–67 points of overconfidence), so description poisoning and drift are calibration attacks as well as selection attacks.
- **Audit, forward:** the bound act record defined by need here is core audit substrate; adjudicability is the shared deliverable.
